## Why use a common template

We'll generate many scenarios as part of requirements elicitation. Since there will be so many, it's important they all work from a common theme or template, so every scenario shares the same characteristics and format. These shared features let us link scenarios to each other, and to the goals and requirements/functions derived from them (traceability).

## Common template fields

Some common fields found in a scenario template:

- **Unique identifier** — a number or combination/sequence uniquely identifying the scenario
- **Title**
- **Author**, **version**, and **change history**
- **Configuration owner**
- **Links or references to the goal(s)** the scenario fulfills
- **Actors**
- **Priority level**
- Preconditions and postconditions
- The scenario steps or narrative itself
- **References to other scenarios** — main scenarios, sub-scenarios, alternatives, or exceptions
- **Forward links** to the requirements and functions derived from this scenario

It's perfectly fine to add to or remove from this list according to your own organization's needs. It's also normal to fill out only part of the template at a time, with more information discovered and added to the fields as the system's life cycle progresses.

As covered in the previous lesson, the best format for a template like this is the table format.

> Bottom line: a shared template across all scenarios enables traceability between scenarios, goals, and requirements — and its fields can be tailored to your organization's needs.
